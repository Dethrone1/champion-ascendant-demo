THE ASCENT SCORE

Professional fighters get signed on somebody's opinion. ASCENT scores them instead, ranking an unsigned fighter against the measured profile of the people who actually got picked. It uses only what anyone can look up: win rate, how often he finishes fights, who he's been fighting for, his age.

There are two separate systems, because there are two main routes into the sport's top tier. ONE Championship is a major promotion that signs fighters directly. The Contender Series is the UFC's televised tryout show. They look for different things, so they get different formulas built on different data, and the two are never mixed.

Why These Factors

WHAT DRIVES THE SCORE

Every component traces back to something measured in the signing or invite data, including the ones that don't work. Full derivation, sources and limits are on the method page.

ONE CHAMPIONSHIP ASCENT (v3.7.1)

up to 65 pts

Finish Rate (Normalized)

The dominant component. Normalized against global division averages (FightMatrix, all promotions): a heavyweight finishing 80% is average for his division, a strawweight doing it is exceptional. Normalization puts them on one scale.

up to 15 pts

Win Rate

Winning matters less than how you win. A decision-heavy undefeated record gets credit, just not as much as a finish-heavy one. ONE leans toward exciting.

0–14 pts

Promotion Pipeline

Calibrated on 2023–present signings. ONE Friday Fights and LFA lead at 12 pts each, Pancrase 6, ACA and Shooto 4. PromotionBase plus an HQ region bonus for where ONE's scouts actually sign from.

2–6 pts

Origin Region Fit

Each region's actual share of 2023–present signings. East Asia and South America clear 15% (+6). North America, Central Asia, the Middle East and Eastern Europe sit at 8%+ (+4). Everywhere else +2. Real shares, not anyone's idea of "appeal."

-4 to +3 pts

Age (Development Runway)

Under 24 gets +3, 24–27 gets +2, 28–30 neutral, 31–33 gets -2, over 34 gets -4. Not a claim about ability. Promotions invest in longer timelines.

0 or 3+W pts

Title Fight Wins

3 + number of title fight wins, uncapped. One title win is +4, four is +7, none is nothing. Rewards proven form at the top of a fighter's current promotion.

DWCS ASCENT (v2.8.5)

up to 45 pts

Finish Rate (Normalized)

The strongest single component. Normalized against global division finish rates (FightMatrix, N=261,967 fights) rather than the invitee pool, which is already selected for finishing. A heavyweight finishing 80% is average; a women's strawweight doing it is exceptional.

up to 15 pts

Win Rate

Winning matters but doesn't dominate. The sub-80% penalty was removed in v2.8.5 because win rate was already counted four ways, and the thresholds punished 78.6% three points harder than 80.0%.

0–15 pts

Win Streak

A square-root curve, so the first few wins carry the most weight. Against the invite target it scores below chance (AUC 0.485). It earns its keep against contract outcomes instead.

3–15 pts

Promotion Pipeline

Each promotion's measured share of verified Season 9 invitees. LFA alone accounts for 21% (15 pts); Fury FC and Cage Warriors 6% each (11 pts). At this sample size two invitees moves a promotion a whole tier, so treat the tiers as ordinal.

-8 to +6 pts

Age

Derived from the age distribution of actual invitees. They cluster at 27–30 (64% of the field), so that band scores highest and fighters under 25 take a penalty. It measures who gets invited, not who has the most career left.

uncapped

Titles & Stack Bonuses

A promotion-gated curve: at a ranked feeder, +5 for the belt, +5 for the first defense, +3 after. Stack bonuses add for undefeated (+5), elite finish rate (+4), perfect finish rate (+3), 7+ streak (+3). Nothing is capped, so scores can pass 100.
