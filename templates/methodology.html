How the score is built

Two formulas, one for each pathway, each built on its own set of real outcomes. There's no trained model and nothing hidden. It's arithmetic you can redo by hand, and the code is public.

Scouting runs on opinion

Ask why a regional fighter deserves a shot and you get adjectives back. Exciting. Durable. Ready. Whoever is saying it usually has a stake in the answer, and there's no way to hold two of those claims up against each other when the fighters are on different continents beating different people.

So I built a number instead. It only uses things anyone can look up: record, finish rate adjusted for division, which promotion he fights for, his age, whether he's won title fights. Those get measured against the profile of the fighters who actually got signed or invited.

A good matchmaker will still beat it. What it does is treat every fighter the same way and show the arithmetic.

From record to published score

  1. Collect

    Fight lists pulled per fighter from Sherdog, then confirmed on Tapology.

  2. Verify

    Where the two disagree, Tapology wins and the other value is noted. Promotions resolve to fixed codes.

  3. Score

    The Python formulas are the source of truth. The version is stored next to every score.

  4. Test

    Every published score is recomputed from its inputs, and the website's calculator is checked against the Python.

  5. Publish

    The board updates. Any change to a published number is logged with a date on Off the Board.

The test step replaced an older check where a spreadsheet had to agree with the script. The spreadsheet still exists for working the data, but the code is what publishes, and the code is what gets tested. Details are on How it's built.

ONE ASCENT v3.9

Benchmarks an unsigned fighter against the fighters ONE Championship has signed to MMA contracts since 2023, scored on their record on the day they signed. Nothing after signing is used, and the formula is never tuned on how signees did once they joined ONE. v3.9 is in validation: published on the board, with further changes held for an end-of-year review.

// ONE ASCENT v3.9 · floored at 0, no upper cap
Score     = Base80 + Promotion + PromotionRegion + Origin + Age + Title + Undefeated
Base80    = 0.65 × FR_norm + 0.15 × WinRate
FR_shrunk = (FinishRate × Wins + 5 × DivisionAvg) / (Wins + 5)
FR_norm   = min(100, FR_shrunk / DivisionAvg × 73)
PartPointsWhy it's there
Finish rateup to 65

The strongest signal in the signing data. Two adjustments: it's shrunk toward the division average as if the fighter had five more fights at that average, so a 3-0 record can't max out on three lucky finishes; then it's normalized by the global finish rate for his division (FightMatrix), because a heavyweight finishing 80% is ordinary and a women's strawweight doing it is exceptional.

Win rateup to 15

Counts, but doesn't run the score. ONE signs finishers.

Promotion0 to 12

Set by the promotion's share of ONE's MMA signings since 2023, in steps of 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12. The cutoffs are shares, not counts, so the tiers keep working as more signings come in. Right now ONE Friday Fights and LFA are at 12, Pancrase and Shooto at 8, ACA and Hardcore MMA at 6. A promotion with no signings scores 0.

Promotion region0 to 2

Share of signings by the promotion's home region: 15% and up earns 2, 10% earns 1, 5% earns 0.5. Southeast Asia and East Asia lead. Kept separate from the promotion points because the two barely correlate (+0.16).

Origin region2 to 6

Each region's real share of signings by where the fighter is from. East Asia and South America earn 6; North America, Central Asia, the Middle East and Eastern Europe earn 4; everywhere else 2. Real shares, not anyone's idea of marketability.

Age−4 to +3

23 and under +3, 24 to 27 +2, 28 to 30 neutral, 31 to 33 −2, 34 and over −4. Promotions invest in longer runways. Not a claim about ability. No age on record scores 0.

Title wins3 + 1 per title

Pro titles only, uncapped. One title is +4, two is +5.

Undefeated+5

No losses and at least five wins. Draws don't break it.
The source of truth is scoring/one_ascent_v3_9.py. A plain-English rule sheet covers the data rules: main promotion is the promotion of the most recent fight, a fighter needs at least four pro fights and an exact date of birth to be listed, and fighters signed or booked with the UFC, ONE, PFL, RIZIN or KSW are off the board.

DWCS ASCENT v3.0

Benchmarks a fighter against the measured profile of Contender Series invitees from Seasons 9 and 10. The target is the invitation. Not the fight result, and not the contract.

// DWCS ASCENT v3.0 · uncapped
Raw     = 45 × FR_norm / 109.9 + 6 × Win% + LossBand + Streak + Promotion + Age + Title + Elite + HotStreak
FR_norm = min(FinishRate / DivisionAvg, 1.55) × 70.9
Streak  = 5.5 × √wins_in_a_row − 4.5
Title   = (1.5 + Promotion / 3) × √title_wins
Score   = 0.9104 × Raw + 19.98      // invitee median lands at 78, 97th percentile at 100
PartPointsWhy it's there
Finish rateup to 45

The strongest single signal against invites (AUC 0.637). Normalized against global division finish rates rather than the invitee pool: invitees are already selected for finishing, so normalizing by them would push everyone toward the ceiling. The ratio caps at 1.55× instead of clamping at 100, so the top of the board still ranks.

Win rateup to 6

Cut from 15 in v3.0. Every invitee wins, so a continuous percentage can't separate a group selected on winning.

Loss band8, 7, 2, 0

Replaced the undefeated bonus. Among invitees, zero and one loss are nearly the same; the drop comes at two.

Win streaksquare root

Scores below chance against invites (AUC 0.485). It looked like the strongest variable when tested against contracts, which was the wrong target. Its weight was not raised.

Promotion3 to 15

Each promotion's share of verified Season 9 invitees. LFA alone produced about a fifth. Unlisted regional shows score 3, not 0: a longer shot, not a dead end. At this sample size two invitees move a promotion a whole tier, so read tiers as ordinal.

Age−3 to +3

26 to 29 earns 3, 23 to 25 and 30 to 31 are neutral, everyone else loses 3. Invite share says older, outcomes say younger; when two signals disagree, near-flat is the honest answer.

Title winsuncapped

Scaled by the promotion's tier, with diminishing returns per belt. A belt at a show that sends fighters to the UFC counts for more.

Bonuses+4, +3

Elite finish rate (normalized 85 or more) and a win streak of seven or more.
The age term still leans against very young fighters. A 22-year-old loses 3 points that a 27-year-old gains. That's right if what you're describing is who actually gets invited, and it feels wrong on something called a prospect board. Flagged here so nobody runs into it and assumes the formula is broken.

What counts as a hit

The DWCS score describes an invite profile, so it has to be judged against the invitation, not whether the fighter wins once he's on the show. A fighter can get invited and lose in ninety seconds and the score was still right about the only thing it claimed. That raises the bar: an invitation only counts as evidence if the score was locked and dated before the booking was public.

What the frozen column shows. Across the whole Season 10 field, v2.7.1 averaged 70.9 against 72.0 for Season 9. About a point apart on a season the weights weren't built from. That's a calibration result: the formula describes an invitee field consistently from one year to the next. It doesn't depend on pre-registration, because nothing about it is a prediction.

What it doesn't show, and I got this wrong at first. Five board fighters turned up on the Season 10 roster, which reads like a hit rate several times the base rate. Then I checked the dates. Most of the roster was public before the board was scored, and all five were already booked. I wasn't picking them; I was scoring people I already knew the answer for. Clean prospective calls: zero. Season 11 gets the clean version: board frozen and published before any casting news.

What broke, and how it was caught

A methodology page with no failures on it isn't worth much.

Leakage

A column that had seen the answers

An experimental variant hit AUC 0.72 against contract outcomes. It looked great until I remembered I'd tuned it after those outcomes were visible. I deleted the column instead of publishing it.

Contamination

Post-signing data in the calibration set

A fighter has to be scored on the record he had the day he signed. One showcase fight on a big card doesn't make that promotion his home either; counting it that way inflates his promotion points.

Silent drift

Stored values that stopped matching the formula

The ONE workbook had component scores typed in as numbers instead of formulas. Over time 26 rows had stale promotion points and 13 had stale age and title values. Every component is a live formula now, reading one shared lookup table.

Name matching

Full names where the table keys on codes

Looking promotions up by display name instead of code quietly dropped most fighters to the unlisted default and moved scores by up to 12 points. Promotion codes are now fixed keys, with the readable name kept separately.

Documentation

Comments that contradicted the code

A docstring described an age modifier roughly ten points away from what the function returned. The code was right, so the comment was rewritten to match it.

Deployment

A calculator that shipped broken

A page update on 24 Oct lost most of the calculator's script, and nothing checked the live page. It's rebuilt from a shared scoring file, and a test now runs it against the Python on more than 8,000 inputs.

Caps discard information

Both formulas run uncapped. Take a fighter who's undefeated, finishes everyone, and holds a belt at a promotion the UFC actually pulls from. Under a cap he looks identical to someone with only one of those three going for him. That's a real difference, and a cap throws it away. Above 100 just means several parts of the profile are elite at once, the same idea as OPS+ in baseball.

I've also cut parts that turned out to be doing nothing. A "perfect finisher" bonus could never fire on its own, because a perfect finish rate already clears the elite threshold, so it came out.

What this does not claim

There's no control group. I have the fighters who got signed or invited. I don't have the ones who were looked at and passed over, because nobody publishes that list. So every weight here describes what a signee or invitee looks like, not what separates them from someone who got turned down. That's the gap between a benchmark and a prediction, and it's why the word "predicts" doesn't appear on this site.

LimitDetail
Small samplesBoth formulas are calibrated on small samples. A couple of signings or invites can move a promotion a whole tier. That boundary is noise and is treated as noise.
Selection circularityThe board scores well above the invitee median, but it was built by selecting on the same things the score measures. That gap comes from how the board was assembled, not evidence that the formula beats matchmakers.
Hand-picked boardBoard fighters were chosen, not sampled, so they can't be used as a comparison group against signees.
Not an outcome modelThe score says nothing about who wins on fight night. Season 9 winners and losers came out even.
Partial leakageDWCS age bands include Season 10 fighters, so the Season 10 freeze is clean on every part except that one.
Confounded age signalThere are simply more 27 to 30 year olds competing, which inflates their invite share. The term ignores conversion rate.

Champion Ascendant

A benchmark, not a prediction. ASCENT describes how closely a fighter matches the record profile of real signings and invites. It doesn't say who will get signed.
