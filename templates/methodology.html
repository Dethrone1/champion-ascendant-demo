HOW THE SCORE IS BUILT

Two formulas, one for each pathway, each built on its own set of verified outcomes. There's no model in here and nothing is fitted. It's arithmetic you can redo by hand if you want to. Nothing goes on the site until the spreadsheet and the Python script land on the same number.

The problem

SCOUTING RUNS ON OPINION

Ask why a regional fighter deserves a shot and you get adjectives back. Exciting. Durable. Ready. Whoever is saying it usually has a stake in the answer. And there's no way to hold two of those claims up against each other when the fighters are on different continents beating different people.

So I built a number instead. It only uses things anyone can look up: win rate, finish rate adjusted for division, which promotion he fights for, his age, whether he's won title fights. Those get measured against the profile of the fighters who actually got signed or invited. A good matchmaker will still beat it. What it does is treat every fighter the same way and show the arithmetic.

Pipeline

SOURCE TO PUBLISHED SCORE

01

Collect

Records pulled per fighter from Tapology, the canonical source. Sherdog secondary for gaps.

02

Verify

Every record cross-checked. Promotion names resolved to canonical codes against a 90-row registry.

03

Score

Python reference implementation. Formula version is stored alongside every score.

04

Parity gate

The workbook recomputes independently. Any disagreement blocks publication until resolved.

05

Publish

Board updates. Corrections go on the public log with a date, never silently.

Step 4 is the one that saves me. The workbook and the script read the same lookup tables but work the numbers out separately, so if either one has drifted they stop agreeing. Right now every scored row reproduces on both boards. One mismatch and nothing publishes until I've found out why.

The formulas

TWO SCORES, NEVER COMBINED

ONE and the Contender Series are looking for different fighters, so they get separate formulas built on separate data. A ONE score and a DWCS score aren't the same unit. I don't average them, I don't merge the boards, and they never share a column. A fair number of the rules on this project exist only to stop that happening by accident.

// ONE ASCENT v3.7.1 — calibrated on 37 verified signings, 2023–present Score = MIN(100, Base80 + PromoPoints + OriginFit + AgeMod + TitleBonus) Base80 = 100 × (0.65 × FR_norm + 0.15 × WinRate) FR_norm = MIN(100, FinishRate / DivisionAvg × 70.9) // DWCS ASCENT v2.8.5 — calibrated on 165 verified invitees, Seasons 9–10 Score = MAX(0, FR45 + Win15 + Streak + Promo + AgeMod + Title + Stack) // deliberately uncapped — see "caps discard information" below
ComponentWeightWhy it's there
Finish rate45 / 80 pts The strongest signal in both datasets by some distance. Normalized against global division finish rates (FightMatrix, N=261,967 fights) rather than against the invitee pool. Invitees are already selected for finishing, so normalizing by them would shove every score up toward the ceiling.
Win rate15 pts Matters, but it isn't allowed to run the score. I removed a sub-80% penalty in v2.8.5 because win rate was already getting counted four different ways, and the step thresholds hit a guy at 78.6% three points harder than one at 80.0%.
Promotion3–15 pts Based on each promotion's measured share of verified invitees rather than its reputation. LFA on its own produced 21% of Season 9 invitees. Unlisted regional shows score 3 instead of 0, since a fighter nobody has seen yet is a longer shot but not a dead end.
Age−8 to +6 Derived from the age distribution of actual invitees, not hand-set. See the caveat below.
Win streak0–15 pts Square-root curve. This one scores below chance against the DWCS invite target (AUC 0.485). It does earn its keep when you point it at contract outcomes instead. I've left it in and flagged it rather than dropping it quietly.
Title fightsuncapped Promotion-gated curve. A belt at a ranked feeder counts for more than a belt at a show that has never sent anyone up.

The age term punishes young fighters. Contender Series invitees bunch up between 27 and 30, so a 27-year-old picks up 6 points and a 22-year-old drops 6. That's right if what you're describing is who actually gets invited, and it feels wrong on something called a prospect board. I'm flagging it up front because anyone who runs into it on their own is going to assume the formula is broken.

Validation

WHAT COUNTS AS A HIT

This score describes an invite profile, so the thing it has to be judged against is the invitation. Not whether the fighter wins once he's on the show. A guy can get invited and lose in ninety seconds and the score was still right about the only thing it claimed. Fight results test something the formula never set out to measure.

That raises the bar rather than lowering it. An invitation only counts as evidence if the score was locked and dated before that booking became public.

What the frozen column does show. Scored across the whole Season 10 field, v2.7.1 produced a mean of 70.9 against 72.0 for Season 9. About a point apart on a season the weights weren't built from. That's a calibration result: the formula describes an invitee field consistently from one year to the next. It's worth having and it doesn't depend on pre-registration, because nothing about it is a prediction.

What it doesn't show, and I got this wrong at first. Five fighters from the prospect board turned up on the Season 10 roster. That reads like a hit rate several times the base rate, and I was ready to write it up that way.

Then I checked the dates. Every prospect carries a Date Scored of 2026-07-31, and 52 of the 63 Season 10 fighters were public by 29 July. All five sit in episodes 1, 2 and 7, announced well before the freeze, and their Invited / Outcome field already read “DWCS S10” when they were scored. I wasn't picking them. I was scoring people I already knew the answer for.

So that is not a hit rate. Clean prospective invitation calls right now: zero.

The fix is slow rather than complicated. Season 10 isn't fully booked, so the board as it stands today is frozen and dated against the slots nobody has seen. Any board fighter appearing in a booking announced after that timestamp is a real call, and if none do, that goes on the record too. Season 11 gets the clean version: board frozen before any casting news, denominator fixed in advance, then count.

Failure log

WHAT BROKE, AND HOW IT WAS CAUGHT

Every one of these got caught by a check I'd built for that exact purpose. I'm listing them because a methodology page with no failures on it isn't worth much.

Leakage

A column that had seen the answers

An experimental variant hit AUC 0.72 against contract outcomes. It looked great until I remembered I'd tuned it after those outcomes were already visible. I deleted the column instead of publishing it. When a number that good turns up that easily, usually something is wrong with how you got there.

Contamination

Post-signing data in the calibration set

A fighter has to be scored on the record he had the day he signed. Anything after that date leaks the answer back into the inputs. The same rule covers showcase appearances: one fight on a big card doesn't make that promotion his home, and counting it that way inflates his pipeline score.

Silent drift

Stored values that stopped matching the formula

The ONE workbook had its component scores typed in as plain numbers instead of formulas. Over time they stopped matching the script: 26 rows with stale promotion points, 13 with stale age and title values, 5 with stale Base80. Every component is a live formula now, all of them pulling from one shared lookup table, so it can't quietly come apart again.

Name matching

Full names where the table keys on codes

Looking promotions up by their display name instead of the short code quietly dropped most fighters to the unlisted default and moved scores by as much as 12 points. It's the reason the promotion registry runs to 90 rows and lists every alias I've come across. The parity check caught this one within about a minute.

Documentation

Comments that contradicted the code

A docstring claimed the age modifier ran +3/+6/+8/+16/+12. The function actually returned −6/−4/−2/+6/+2. Roughly ten points between what the comment said and what the code did. The code was the correct one, so I rewrote the comment to match it.

Design decisions

CAPS DISCARD INFORMATION

I took out three caps: one on the total, one on the title bonus, one on win streak. All three were squashing genuinely different fighters into the same number at the top end. Take a guy who's undefeated, finishes everyone he beats, and holds a belt at a promotion the UFC actually pulls from. Under a cap he comes out looking identical to someone who has one of those three going for him. That's a real difference and the cap was throwing it in the bin.

So DWCS scores run uncapped and a few of them go past 100. Above 100 just means several parts of the profile are elite at once. The display scale is anchored so the invitee median sits around the middle of the range instead of everyone piling up against a ceiling. Same idea as OPS+ in baseball.

I've also cut things that turned out to be doing nothing. A "perfect finisher" bonus was arithmetically redundant with the finish-rate term already sitting in the formula, so it came out. Age bands went from two-year steps down to three wide ones after the finer version tested worse.

Limits

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM

There's no control group. I have the fighters who got signed or invited. I don't have the ones who got looked at and passed over, because nobody publishes that list. So every weight in here is describing what an invitee looks like. None of them describe what separates an invitee from someone who got turned down. That's the gap between a benchmark and a prediction, and it's why the word "predicts" doesn't show up anywhere on this site.

LimitationDetail
Small samples Both formulas are calibrated on small samples. On the Season 9 promotion tiers, two invitees are enough to move a promotion a whole tier. That boundary is noise and it's written up as noise in the source.
Partial leakage The age bands include Season 10 fighters, which means the Season 10 pre-registration is clean on every component except that one. Worth knowing before you read too much into it.
Selection circularity The public boards score well above the invitee median, but I built those boards by selecting on the same things the score measures. The gap comes from how the board was assembled. It isn't evidence that the formula beats real matchmakers.
Not an outcome model The score says nothing at all about who wins on fight night. Season 9 winners and losers came out effectively even.
Confounded age signal Invitee age share is confounded by the underlying population, since there are simply more 27 to 30 year olds competing in the first place. The term also ignores conversion rate completely.

Season 10 results come in through autumn 2026. That's the first real prospective test this thing gets, and the frozen v2.7.1 column is what I'll be marking it against. However it turns out, that column stays exactly as it is.
